P	From its early days in the 52d Signal Battalion, SSD's reputation for rapid deployment and technology innovation became hallmarks. The unit was outfitted with the available conventional signal capabilities of the time - HF radio, teletype systems, and the earliest UHF Tactical Satellite radio systems. These systems were not designed for small team, rapid deployment. With the support and assistance of the SOCEUR J6, the Detachment rapidly adapted their equipment to be modular, more "man-portable," and rapidly transportable on a variety of military and dual-use aircraft. As satellite terminals evolved and the internet became a C2 platform, so too has SSD's ability to find new ways to accomplish its missions in challenging circumstances and austere locations. Ideas born in SSD and SOCEUR J6 have found their way into communications units across the SOF community and into the US Army. Its alumni have served in a variety of specialized and high-visibility communications units across the Department of Defense.
P	In 2012 when Admiral McRaven (as the USSOCOM Commander) decided to standardize the structure of the TSOCs, the SOCEUR Signal Detachment's proven organization served as the model for the other Theater SOC Signal Detachments. Of the seven current TSOC detachments - all now assigned to the 112th Signal Battalion -five follow the 32-Soldier SSD model and the other two are a smaller 15-Soldier variant.
P	SSD continues to provide innovative, ready, and reliable communications teams, supporting various mission profiles. It has been on jump-status continuously since its initial formation in 1985, and this paratrooper spirit motivates its innovative and can-do mindset.
